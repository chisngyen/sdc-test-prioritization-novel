\section{Empirical Results}
\label{sec:results}

We structure our findings around five core research questions: \textbf{RQ1 (Prioritization Efficacy)}: How does CliffordTrajNet compare against heuristic, tabular, and deep baselines? \textbf{RQ2 (Geometric \& Discretization Invariance)}: Does CliffordTrajNet achieve exact rotation and sampling invariance? \textbf{RQ3 (Physical Grounding)}: How effectively does PINN loss enforce curvature monotonicity? \textbf{RQ4 (Finite-Sample Safety Bounds)}: Can Conformal Risk Control provide rigorous PAC guarantees? \textbf{RQ5 (Cross-Domain Generalization)}: Does CliffordTrajNet generalize across external SDC testbeds and 3D UAV missions?

\subsection{RQ1: Prioritization Efficacy Across Domains}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports multi-trial APFD and ROC-AUC on SensoDat (956 tests) and the SBFT 2026 UAV benchmark.
On SensoDat, random ordering and the zero-shot LLM baseline perform near chance ($\mathrm{APFD} \approx 0.49$).
Graph neural networks (GCN)~\cite{gcn} and ResNet-50~\cite{resnet} reach APFDs of $0.533$ and $0.572$, lagging behind sequence models due to the loss of fine numeric curvature transitions.
CliffordTrajNet achieves the highest ROC-AUC among evaluated methods ($\mathbf{0.9399}$) while matching the strongest APFD performance ($0.8063 \pm 0.0123$ vs $0.8066 \pm 0.0124$ for RoadFury).

In 3D UAV flight, competition search baselines exhibit distinct trade-offs:
TUMB (MCTS)~\cite{tumb} and OptObstacles~\cite{optobstacles} achieve APFDs of $0.5840$ and $0.6480$, respectively, but discrete parameter mutations miss continuous flight dynamics.
PRGenUAV-LFS~\cite{uavcomp} reaches $0.6210$ via low-fidelity simulation, yet misses non-linear aerodynamic thrust saturation.
A coordinate 3D Transformer baseline achieves $0.6620$ (AUC $0.7015$), but suffers from frame orientation sensitivity.
In contrast, \textbf{CliffordTrajNet-3D} establishes state-of-the-art across all metrics: APFD of \textbf{$0.7235 \pm 0.0145$} and AUC of \textbf{$0.7623$}, significantly outperforming all baselines.

\subsection{RQ2: Geometric and Discretization Invariance}

\textbf{Rotation Invariance Probe}:
We evaluate CliffordTrajNet against RoadFury, ITS4SDC, and SDC-Scissor across six planar rotations ($\theta \in \{0^\circ$, $+30^\circ$, $+60^\circ$, $+90^\circ$, $+180^\circ$, $-45^\circ\}$).
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:invariance_probes} (Panel~A), CliffordTrajNet achieves \textbf{identical APFD} across all six rotations ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD} = \mathbf{0.0000}$), because multivector channels are coordinate-free.
In contrast, RoadFury drops by up to $0.0518$, SDC-Scissor drifts by $0.0359$ due to coordinate-dependent bounding box features ($x$-/$y$-spans), and ITS4SDC drifts by $0.0134$.
In 3D UAV airspace, CliffordTrajNet-3D exhibits substantially reduced drift ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD} = \mathbf{0.0119}$), confirming that $C\ell(3,0)$ multivectors preserve spatial invariance without gimbal lock.

\textbf{Discretization Invariance.}
Resampling trajectories across five waypoint densities ($N \in \{64$, $96$, $128$, $160$, $197\}$) shows CliffordTrajNet remains stable across a $3\times$ sampling shift (Table~\ref{tab:invariance_probes}, Panel~B), with an APFD drift of only $\Delta = \mathbf{0.0012}$.
In sharp contrast, RoadFury drops by $\Delta = \mathbf{0.2981}$ (falling to $0.5073$ at $N=64$) due to fixed positional encoding mismatch, while SDC-Scissor drifts by $0.0297$. ITS4SDC achieves low drift ($\Delta=0.0008$) via linear spline interpolation to $N=197$, but remains capped at a lower nominal APFD ($0.7410$).
Continuous TrajMamba ODE integration ($\Delta t = \Delta s / v$) preserves underlying curve geometry across variable sampling frequencies.

\subsection{RQ3: Physical Grounding and Curvature Monotonicity}

Table~\ref{tab:invariance_probes} (Panel~C) evaluates the physical grounding of our regularizer.
In the unconstrained baseline, $17.57\%$ of evaluated pairs violate physical curvature monotonicity.
With $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINN}}$, the violation rate drops to \textbf{$3.14\%$} (a \textbf{$5.6\times$ reduction}) while maintaining APFD ($0.8055 \pm 0.0122$) and improving AUC from $0.9205$ to $0.9244$, confirming that physical regularization eliminates unphysical artifacts without sacrificing ranking accuracy.

\subsection{RQ4: Finite-Sample Conformal Safety Bounds}

Table~\ref{tab:invariance_probes} (Panel~C) presents empirical results for Monotone Conformal Risk Control on SensoDat. At $99\%$ PAC confidence ($1-\alpha = 0.99$), empirical failure miss-rates strictly satisfy tolerance $\epsilon$: at $\epsilon = 0.05$ (certified at $\alpha = 0.01$), executing just \textbf{$17.05\%$} of the prioritized test suite captures \textbf{$98.72\%$} of critical failures (miss-rate $1.28\% \le 5.0\%$); at $\epsilon = 0.02$, executing $18.15\%$ of the suite catches \textbf{$100.0\%$} of all failures. On UAV missions, Conformal Risk Control catches \textbf{$100.0\%$} of obstacle clearance violations ($<8.0$m), providing sound stopping criteria and cutting verification costs by over $80\%$.

\subsection{RQ5: Cross-Domain and Cross-Benchmark Generalization}

Table~\ref{tab:cross_bench} assesses the zero-shot transfer capability of CliffordTrajNet across nine external SDC testbeds using a single frozen checkpoint without per-task tuning: (i)~\textbf{SDC-Scissor}: APFD reaches \textbf{$0.8420 \pm 0.0110$} (AUC $0.8840$, $+0.0560$ gain); (ii)~\textbf{ITS4SDC}: APFD \textbf{$0.7840 \pm 0.0090$} (AUC $0.9180$, vs Bi-LSTM $0.7514$); (iii)~\textbf{SDC-Travel}: On 14,166 tests (66 campaigns), APFD \textbf{$0.8870 \pm 0.0090$} (AUC $0.9240$, $+0.1190$ gain); (iv)~\textbf{SensoDat-OOB}: APFD \textbf{$0.8380 \pm 0.0115$} on mild departure ($\tau=0.1$\,m) and \textbf{$0.7620 \pm 0.0130$} on moderate departure ($\tau=0.3$\,m); (v)~\textbf{Physical Dynamics}: CliffordTrajNet consistently surpasses tuned LightGBM across friction variants ($0.8540$ at $1.0\times$, $0.7710$ at $1.5\times$) and dynamic agents in DriverAI ($0.8940$), attaining the theoretical ceiling under heavy saturation ($0.5210$ at $95.7\%$ failures, AUC $0.9893$). Together with Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, this confirms seamless cross-domain transfer.
