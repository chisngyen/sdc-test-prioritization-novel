\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented \textbf{SE2RoadNet}, a geometry- and physics-grounded Transformer for robust test case prioritization in self-driving car simulations.
Addressing the key limitations of existing deep sequence models---namely, vulnerability to coordinate rotation, sensitivity to point-sampling discretization, and unphysical failure predictions---SE2RoadNet introduces a 7-channel coordinate-free differential geometric feature representation paired with a relative-arclength attention bias parameterized by Random Fourier Features.
Furthermore, we embedded vehicle lateral dynamics into training via a physics-informed loss (PINN) that enforces curvature monotonicity.

Extensive empirical evaluations across SensoDat ($32{,}580$ tests) and eight public benchmarks demonstrate that SE2RoadNet achieves:
(i)~provable and bit-identical $\mathrm{SE}(2)$ rotation invariance ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD} = 0.0000$),
(ii)~near-zero discretization drift ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD} \le 0.0012$),
(iii)~a $5.6\times$ reduction in physical curvature violations, and
(iv)~state-of-the-art discriminative power with an AUC of $0.935$--$0.938$ and an APFD of $0.805$--$0.807$.
In future work, we plan to extend SE2RoadNet to closed-loop dynamic traffic environments and explore real-to-sim transfer on physical autonomous vehicle test tracks.
