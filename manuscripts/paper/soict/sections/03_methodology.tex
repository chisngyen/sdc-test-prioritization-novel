\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we present the mathematical formulation and architecture of \textbf{CliffordTrajNet}.
We first define the Clifford Geometric Algebra foundations for 2D and 3D autonomous trajectories, followed by the Continuous TrajMamba state-space model, the physics-informed regularizer, and the monotone conformal risk control mechanism.

\subsection{Clifford Geometric Algebra Formulation}

Let an autonomous agent trajectory be represented as an ordered sequence of spatial waypoints $\mathcal{P} = \{\mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_L\}$, where $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ for ground vehicles and $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ for aerial drones.
Traditional neural networks map Cartesian coordinates through Euclidean affine transformations $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{p}_i + \mathbf{b}$, which break under rigid rotations $\mathbf{R} \in \mathrm{SO}(d)$.

In contrast, Clifford Geometric Algebra $C\ell(p, q)$ equips a vector space with a geometric product combining inner (symmetric) and outer (antisymmetric) products:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u} \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u} \wedge \mathbf{v}.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2D Ground Domain}: In $C\ell(2,0)$, the algebra has dimension $2^2 = 4$, spanned by $\{1, e_1, e_2, e_{12}\}$, where $e_{12} = e_1 \wedge e_2$ represents the planar bivector (yaw rotation plane). A displacement vector $\Delta \mathbf{p} = \Delta x e_1 + \Delta y e_2$ is mapped to a multivector:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{2D}} = \|\Delta\mathbf{p}\|_2 + \Delta x e_1 + \Delta y e_2 + \kappa(s) e_{12}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{3D Aerial Domain}: In $C\ell(3,0)$, the algebra has dimension $2^3 = 8$, spanned by scalar $1$, three vectors $\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$, three bivectors $\{e_{12}, e_{23}, e_{31}\}$ (yaw, roll, pitch planes), and a pseudoscalar $e_{123} = e_1 e_2 e_3$ (volume/helicity).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Rotor Sandwich and Spatial Invariance}:
Spatial rotations in $C\ell(d, 0)$ are represented by even-grade multivectors called \emph{rotors} $R = \exp(-\frac{\theta}{2} B)$, where $B$ is a unit bivector.
A vector $v$ is transformed via the rotor sandwich:
\begin{equation}
v' = R v R^\dagger,
\end{equation}
where $R^\dagger$ is the reverse of $R$.
In $C\ell(3,0)$, the rotor algebra is strictly isomorphic to unit quaternions $\mathbb{H}$, performing 3D spatial rotations without gimbal lock.
Because geometric products and norms are invariant under orthogonal group actions, the resulting feature representations satisfy exact planar $SE(2)$ invariance and low $SE(3)$ sensitivity.

\subsection{Continuous TrajMamba State-Space Model}

To process variable-resolution waypoints with robust discretization tolerance, we design a continuous Selective State Space Model (\textbf{TrajMamba})~\cite{mamba}.
Given continuous arclength parameter $s$, the trajectory dynamics satisfy the linear differential equation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}h(s)}{\mathrm{d}s} = \mathbf{A} h(s) + \mathbf{B}(s) x(s), \quad y(s) = \mathbf{C}(s) h(s) + \mathbf{D} x(s),
\end{equation}
where $x(s) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ is the projected Clifford multivector and $h(s) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{state}}}$ is the latent memory.

Discretizing along the physical arclength step $\Delta s_i = \|\mathbf{p}_{i+1} - \mathbf{p}_i\|_2$ with local velocity $v_i$ yields the continuous time increment $\Delta t_i = \Delta s_i / \max(v_i, \epsilon)$.
The state transition matrices are computed via zero-order hold (ZOH):
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathbf{A}}_i = \exp(\Delta t_i \mathbf{A}), \quad \overline{\mathbf{B}}_i = \mathbf{A}^{-1}(\overline{\mathbf{A}}_i - \mathbf{I}) \mathbf{B}_i.
\end{equation}
Because the state recurrence integrates along the physical curve arclength $\int \mathrm{d}s$, changing the simulator waypoint sampling rate preserves the underlying continuous trajectory flow, substantially reducing waypoint discretization sensitivity.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Monotonicity Regularization}

In lateral vehicle and drone dynamics, trajectory instability occurs when lateral acceleration demand exceeds physical limits: $a_c(s) = v(s)^2 \kappa(s) > \mu g$.
To enforce that sharper turns monotonically increase predicted hazard, we introduce the physics-informed monotonicity regularizer $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINN}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINN}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{k=1}^{|\mathcal{B}|} \max\left(0, -\frac{\partial \hat{y}_k}{\partial \kappa_{\max, k}}\right) + \lambda_v \max\left(0, \hat{y}_k \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\{\kappa_{\max, k} < \kappa_{\mathrm{safe}}\}} - \epsilon\right).
\end{equation}
The total training objective balances classification and physics: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Focal}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{PINN}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINN}}$.

\subsection{Monotone Conformal Risk Control (PAC Safety Bounds)}

Traditional prioritizers output uncalibrated rank orders with no safety stopping guarantees.
We formulate a finite-sample risk control mechanism based on the Learn-Then-Test (LTT) framework~\cite{ltt}.
Let $S(\mathbf{p}) \in [0, 1]$ denote the predicted failure score.
A testing team executes all test cases whose score exceeds a cutoff threshold $\lambda \in [0, 1]$.
The empirical test loss (missed failure rate) is defined as:
\begin{equation}
L(\lambda) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\{S(\mathbf{p}_i) < \lambda\}}}{\max(1, \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i)},
\end{equation}
where $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates simulation failure.
Because $L(\lambda)$ is non-decreasing with respect to $\lambda$, we invert Hoeffding-Bentkus concentration bounds~\cite{bentkus} on a calibration set of $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ scenarios to find the optimal threshold $\hat{\lambda}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\lambda} = \sup \left\{ \lambda \in [0, 1] : \mathrm{HB\_Bound}\left(\widehat{R}(\lambda), n_{\mathrm{cal}}\right) \le \epsilon \right\}.
\end{equation}
This establishes a finite-sample probably approximately correct (PAC) guarantee:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}[L(\hat{\lambda})] \le \epsilon\right) \ge 1 - \alpha,
\end{equation}
guaranteeing that executing the prioritized subset $S(\mathbf{p}) \ge \hat{\lambda}$ catches at least $(1 - \epsilon)$ of all safety violations with $(1 - \alpha)$ confidence.
