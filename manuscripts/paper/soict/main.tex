\begin{abstract}
Simulation-based testing is critical for assuring the safety and reliability of
autonomous cyber-physical systems (ACPS), including autonomous driving systems
(ADS) and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). However, executing high-fidelity
simulations across large operational design domains is computationally expensive
under tight release cycles. Test case prioritization (TCP) addresses this cost by
ordering tests so that failure-revealing scenarios are executed earlier. Existing
heuristic and deep learning prioritizers remain sensitive to coordinate-frame
rotations and waypoint sampling, while providing no finite-sample control over
missed failures.

We propose \textbf{CliffordTrajNet}, a geometric deep learning and continuous
state-space framework for autonomous-system test prioritization. First,
CliffordTrajNet represents spatial trajectories using Clifford geometric algebra
$C\ell(p,q)$---$C\ell(2,0)$ for 2D road trajectories and $C\ell(3,0)$ for 3D
flight trajectories---with multivector representations and rotor-based
operations for geometrically structured spatial encoding without Euler-angle
singularities. Second, TrajMamba uses arclength-aware continuous state-space
dynamics to substantially reduce sensitivity to waypoint resampling. Third, a
physics-informed monotonicity regularizer penalizes hazard predictions that
conflict with lateral-acceleration constraints. Fourth, conformal risk control
provides finite-sample statistical control of missed-failure risk under
constrained execution budgets. Across the large-scale SensoDat simulation
benchmark ($32{,}580$ tests), nine external SDC testbeds, and the official SBFT
2026 UAV benchmark, CliffordTrajNet produces identical APFD across the tested
planar rotations ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD}=0.0000$), low resolution-induced APFD
drift ($\Delta\mathrm{APFD}\leq0.0012$), an ROC-AUC of $0.9399$ and APFD of
$0.8063$ on SDC, and an APFD of $0.7235$ on UAV testing.

\keywords{Autonomous Driving Testing \and UAV Testing \and Test Case

\end{abstract}
